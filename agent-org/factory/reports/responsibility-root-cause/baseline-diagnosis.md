# Baseline diagnosis and observed control escape

This is sanitized organization evidence from an observed correction, not a fictional passing experiment or an independently authenticated transcript. Project identity, raw conversation, paths and product decisions remain with their owner. No public original-message URL is available; the Coordinator's incident account and preserved prior policies support this bounded diagnosis. Fresh behavioral replays are separate observations and may pass even with baseline instructions.

## Observed causal sequence

1. A broad application-completion instruction and a compatibility outcome were treated as continuing build authority.
2. A supported dependency could not meet the outcome. The proposed response added ownership of unsupported platform behavior and a custom compatibility subsystem.
3. Successive small research/implementation slices retained the same feature/target framing; the Coordinator recorded that the original completion instruction persisted.
4. Technical/design readiness review accepted bounded implementation conditions. The classification of the added engineering responsibility was not rejected; implementation delegation continued.
5. The operator interrupted and explicitly rejected the expansion. Dependent workers stopped. Project-specific containment was recorded separately; no claim that this repaired the general organization weakness was made.

This is an observed failure of admission and review despite existing rules requiring outcome/authority fit. It does not prove all agents or all tasks fail, nor that any particular sentence alone caused the behavior.

## Causal diagnosis

Proximate decision error: unchanged user-visible outcome was mistaken for unchanged implementation scope. Control escape: task/plan restatements of broad authority and technical readiness reinforced the same premise rather than independently establishing authority for the new responsibility. Systemic weakness: the boundary between routine implementation and new maintenance/operational responsibility was discretionary, without an operational before/after comparison or aggregate-child check.

Contributing conditions: strong persistence after admission, detailed procedures for evidence/implementation continuation, and local requirements that constrained product behavior more explicitly than upstream maintenance obligations. These amplify a wrong admission; they are not independent authority and do not justify reducing ordinary autonomy or removing existing quality gates.

Alternative explanations: the recorded scope might genuinely have included the new responsibility (not established by the available instruction); generic authority rules might be sufficient when correctly applied (plausible, hence baseline replay successes must be retained); instruction volume or model tendencies might contribute (unmeasured, not asserted roots). Five whys identifies a causal chain, not proof of hidden model motivations.

Counterfactual test: with the proposed boundary applied at step2, the dependency-support mismatch produces standard alternatives plus an explicit coverage gap and a scope decision, before custom implementation/probes. Requiring a reviewer to reconstruct the responsibility delta at step4 provides an independent second opportunity to reject the expansion.
