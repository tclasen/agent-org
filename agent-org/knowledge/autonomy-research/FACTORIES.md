# Software factories and outcome evaluation

Research observed 22 October 2026 UTC (21 October Pacific), for TASK-000058 / REQ-000051. Local comparison: `8916d3589054a6416098598a029d64c1d676dd7f`. This is research and proposed experiments only. No evaluator, authority, runtime, service or project behavior changes are adopted.

The strongest transferable idea is to specify observable outcomes before decomposing implementation, then verify candidate artifacts using independently controlled cases and a clean environment. Agent-org already has independent roles, pinned evidence, preregistration and promotion gates. Its useful increment is better behavioral evidence at the final workflow consumer, rather than another orchestration layer. Public factory reports motivate experiments; they do not establish that unattended delivery is safe here.

## What the primary systems demonstrate

### StrongDM software factory and official Attractor specifications

StrongDM's 6 February 2026 account describes non-interactive development driven by specifications and scenarios, without human code writing or review. Its scenarios represent user stories, often outside the implementation repository as holdouts. “Satisfaction” estimates the fraction of observed trajectories likely to satisfy users. The team reports behavioral twins of Okta, Jira, Slack and Google applications, enabling high-volume failure testing without live-service limits. These are first-party operating claims; the article supplies neither the complete factory implementation nor independently audited fidelity, incident rates or costs. [StrongDM account](https://factory.strongdm.ai/).

The distinct, official [`strongdm/attractor`](https://github.com/strongdm/attractor/tree/fb57a55ed97372a27ac90102f436947e29f48426) repository contains three natural-language specifications and a README asking an agent to implement them. The inspected root contains no executable factory implementation. It must not be confused with third-party repositories sharing the name. [Official README](https://github.com/strongdm/attractor/blob/fb57a55ed97372a27ac90102f436947e29f48426/README.md).

The graph specification describes DOT workflows with typed handlers, conditional edges, parallel branches, bounded node retries, checkpoints after completed nodes and human decision gates. Goal gates accept `SUCCESS` or `PARTIAL_SUCCESS`; consequently their presence alone does not prove our stricter acceptance criteria. Checkpoints restore graph context but cannot serialize live LLM sessions: the first resumed hop may use a summary. The coding-loop specification separately describes steering, output truncation, tool timeouts and loop warnings. Sandbox support is an extension point, and environment-variable filtering explicitly is not a security boundary. These are specified behaviors, not locally exercised guarantees. [Graph specification](https://github.com/strongdm/attractor/blob/fb57a55ed97372a27ac90102f436947e29f48426/attractor-spec.md), [coding-loop specification](https://github.com/strongdm/attractor/blob/fb57a55ed97372a27ac90102f436947e29f48426/coding-agent-loop-spec.md).

**Local implication:** borrow external behavior cases and explicit resume semantics. Retain independent approval, inspectable implementation and remote delivery verification. A directory outside a repository remains readable on a shared host; a genuine holdout needs enforced access separation. Repeatedly optimizing against a disclosed score also gradually turns the holdout into training feedback. Twin fidelity is another independent hypothesis, not a consequence of scenario volume.

### OpenAI internal harness engineering experiment

Ryan Lopopolo's 11 February 2026 engineering account reports an internal product built with agent-written application code, tests, tooling and documentation. Relevant mechanisms are worktree-specific application instances and ephemeral logs/metrics, a short repository entrypoint linking structured knowledge, mechanical architecture checks, and feedback converted into documentation or tooling. The team reports about one tenth of estimated manual development time; this is an estimate without a controlled counterfactual in the article. Minimal merge gates and agent-driven merges are contextual practices, not universal recommendations. The author explicitly limits generalization to similarly prepared repositories and identifies long-term architectural coherence as unknown. The internal product/harness source was not provided by this account. [OpenAI engineering report](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).

**Local implication:** agent-org's root entrypoint, `shared/CONTEXT_MANAGEMENT.md`, structured records and checks already overlap substantially. The unmet question is whether agents can observe the behavior they must prove. For organization work, that means authoritative claims, receipts and recovery state; project UI or production observability requires its own scope. The report supports measuring environment readiness before increasing autonomy, without copying its merge philosophy.

### Factory Missions

Factory's 10 April 2026 architecture article describes an orchestrator, fresh implementation workers, scrutiny validators and black-box user-testing validators. A validation contract precedes feature decomposition; each feature claims the assertions it fulfills. Milestone failures generate explicit repair features, followed by validation again. Shared artifacts externalize state. Its Slack-clone example reports 16.5 hours total, with validation consuming 37.2%, and 21 repair features. These are vendor-reported results for one demonstration, with no matched baseline. No Missions runtime source was inspected. [Missions architecture](https://factory.com/news/missions-architecture).

Current documentation adds a crucial prerequisite: the application needs a scriptable way to exercise user flows and dependencies for reliable QA. It also describes parallelism, cost/correctness tradeoffs and error accumulation as open questions. These qualifications weigh against equating additional concurrent workers with better results. [Missions documentation](https://docs.factory.com/missions/overview).

**Local implication:** agent-org already separates Planner, Developer, Reviewer and Chief of Staff, uses fresh executions and requires observable criteria. The useful addition is an assertion-to-feature-to-evidence map, including cross-feature assertions and validation effort. It should extend existing requirement records, not create a competing mission authority or assume that different models ensure independence.

### Harbor

Harbor is executable open-source evaluation infrastructure, distinct from a factory making production changes. Its README identifies it as the official Terminal-Bench 2.0 harness and describes agents, datasets, environments and rollout generation. Inspected verifier code clears stale reward files, uploads selected tests when required, executes the test script, retrieves verifier artifacts, and parses numeric rewards. Empty, missing, malformed or non-finite rewards raise named errors. This is observable source behavior, not proof that every environment backend enforces isolation. [Harbor README](https://github.com/harbor-framework/harbor/blob/d4038de2a85b65350ab923142ecf7a984976a3a2/README.md), [verifier implementation](https://github.com/harbor-framework/harbor/blob/d4038de2a85b65350ab923142ecf7a984976a3a2/src/harbor/verifier/verifier.py).

The verifier uses its supplied environment; that class alone does not create a pristine second sandbox. Late test upload does not remove an agent's earlier changes to interpreters, fixtures or command resolution. **Local implication:** reuse the separation among task definition, agent execution, grading and result parsing as a design pattern. Do not treat a numeric reward or container label as proof of evaluator integrity. Existing `factory/instrumentation/runner.py`, `events.py` and measurement-health reporting cover parts of this already; a second evaluation framework is not justified by this inspection.

### Terminal-Bench 2.0

The 17 January 2026 paper defines 89 challenging terminal tasks, each with an environment, human reference solution and verification tests. This evaluates agents operating tools over realistic tasks, rather than just generated patches. Its reported frontier scores describe the paper's experimental configurations and date, not present capability. This lane inspected the primary paper's abstract and metadata, and Harbor's implementation; it did not audit all 89 task verifiers or run benchmark agents. [Terminal-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868).

**Local implication:** use small, resettable end-to-end tasks for Coordinator behavior, with an executable reference and expected-negative controls. Scores on terminal programming tasks cannot establish correct authority interpretation, honest uncertainty or PR delivery here. A local benchmark needs explicit coverage of those organization outcomes. Public tasks can also be memorized, so they should not become the sole promotion workload.

### SWE-bench Pro V2

The current V2 documentation, modified 22 September 2026, describes 642 validated tasks across 11 repositories, rather than the original 731-task V1 split. Each task includes instructions, environment, reference patch and verifier. The locked protocol restricts agent-phase networking, captures a patch, and grades it in a pristine environment with the unchanged verifier. The maintainers report reference-patch success and empty-patch failure for all 642 tasks, preserving infrastructure retries. Their revision notes describe repaired instructions, parsers, test patches and dependencies. These are release claims; the benchmark was not rerun here. [V2 protocol](https://github.com/scaleapi/SWE-bench_Pro-os/blob/66f92766bba642462d4bbe5479e83f91f9211862/v2/README.md).

Inspected `PatchReplayAgent` code finds a prior task's captured patch, uploads it and attempts application in a new environment; missing/empty patches apply nothing. It logs application return status and uses fallback patch application commands. This confirms a concrete replay mechanism while also motivating inspection of exact application results. Replaying only the exported patch prevents reliance on many transient changes left in the original sandbox; it does not establish that the patch satisfies behavior outside the verifier's coverage. [Replay implementation](https://github.com/scaleapi/SWE-bench_Pro-os/blob/66f92766bba642462d4bbe5479e83f91f9211862/v2/tooling/patch_replay.py).

**Local implication:** verify organization artifacts from a clean, pinned snapshot under retained evaluators. Require no-op and known-good controls before interpreting candidate improvement. The substantial V2 repairs demonstrate why benchmark name, task count and model score without exact release/protocol are insufficient evidence. Keep V1 comparisons separate.

### Sierra τ-bench lineage

The original repository explicitly warns that its tasks are outdated and directs readers to `tau2-bench`, which now carries the τ³ lineage. Current README documentation also records grading changes and version-specific comparability limits. Historical τ-bench results must retain their version and simulator configuration. [Original warning](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau-bench/blob/main/README.md), [current repository](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/05736c27219b769d0be507574dd3135010916644/README.md).

Current evaluation documentation distinguishes reference actions from required actions. Usually reference actions generate a target database state, and any trajectory reaching equivalent state can pass that component. Only components named in `reward_basis` gate final reward; diagnostic action similarity or natural-language assertions may not. Inspected environment evaluator code creates predicted and gold environments, replays reference actions, compares database hashes and multiplies enabled reward components. Current communication scoring can use a semantic judge, so state checking and language judgment must be reported separately. [Evaluation contract](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/05736c27219b769d0be507574dd3135010916644/docs/evaluation.md), [environment evaluator](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/05736c27219b769d0be507574dd3135010916644/src/tau2/evaluator/evaluator_env.py), [reward composition](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/blob/05736c27219b769d0be507574dd3135010916644/src/tau2/evaluator/evaluator.py).

**Local implication:** grade final state and required process separately. Different valid task paths should pass, while authority, independent review and delivery obligations remain hard constraints even if final text looks correct. Conversely, a detailed reference transcript should not become the only permitted route. End-state equality alone can miss an impermissible intermediate action or unsupported completion claim.

## Proposed local experiments, in priority order

The [existing Tessl research](../TESSL_FACTORY_RESEARCH.md) already proposes behavioral cases, context manifests, retained corrections and recovery sequences. The following specifies complementary evaluation mechanisms. Proposed paths below do not exist by virtue of appearing here. Implementation requires separate task admission and prior-rule approval; current policies remain authoritative.

| Priority and owner | Proposed addition and existing overlap | Expected benefit and bounded evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Planner and independent Reviewer | Proposed `agent-org/factory/benchmarks/behavior/CONTRACT.md` and case definitions; connect to existing `policies/PLANNING.md` after approval. Existing requirements/acceptance already define criteria. | Give every assertion initial state, observable outcome, permitted effects, forbidden intermediate behavior and verifier. Test missing cross-feature outcomes, valid alternative paths and false completion reports. Compare accepted-outcome rate and total repair/review effort. |
| 2 — Developer; measurement Reviewer | Proposed `agent-org/factory/instrumentation/artifact_replay.py` and `factory/tests/test_artifact_replay.py`; build on `scope.py`, `runner.py` and pinned quality gates. | Re-evaluate exported candidate artifacts in a disposable clean snapshot under retained evaluator bytes. Seed altered tests, stale rewards, wrong revision and environment-only shims; require rejection or explicit unknown. Known-good controls must pass and empty/no-op fixes must fail on repair cases. |
| 3 — Architect and Researcher; Reviewer validates | Proposed `agent-org/factory/benchmarks/behavior/effect-reconciliation/` fixtures. Existing lifecycle and event records require reconciliation already. | Model lost response after successful effect, duplicate dispatch, stale approval and conflicting claim. Grade final ownership/receipt state plus absence of duplicate effect. A small local fake should expose only required Git/PR-like operations; no real remote writes or daemon. |
| 4 — Coordinator and measurement Reviewer | Proposed `agent-org/factory/reports/validation-coverage/` observations using existing `metrics/scorecards.py` input conventions. | Trace assertion → case → observed evidence → disposition, showing coverage and ungraded assertions. Separate implementation, validation and repair effort; compare total accepted outcome cost with missing telemetry explicit. |

Start with the contract and a small evaluator qualification exercise, then consider replay. A fixture proving evaluator behavior is distinct from evidence that a new agent workflow improves outcomes. For stochastic promotion comparisons, retain `factory/quality-gates/DEFINITION.md`: preregister at least 20 unique paired cases, fixed ordering procedure and stopping rule, the existing confidence/benefit requirements, full failed/retried effort, all hard guardrails and cumulative budget. Twenty cases are a minimum, not adequate power by assertion. If credible resource measurements or representative cases are unavailable, the outcome remains inconclusive.

Holdout construction should be owned independently of the implementer and include unseen compositions of known failures. Shared local files provide procedural separation only. A future implementation must state who can read/write tests and baseline artifacts, what the native sandbox actually enforces and which observations it misses. Begin with fixed tool-result fixtures when technical isolation is unavailable; do not claim hidden evaluation. Avoid LLM judging where deterministic receipts or state comparison suffice. For semantic assertions, preregister labeled positive, negative, ambiguous and adversarial examples and measure errors and abstention before using the judge in a gate.

For effect fixtures, document fidelity limits explicitly: a simulated merge receipt does not demonstrate GitHub branch protection, live connector permissions or remote-main delivery. Use fault injection to test response loss and idempotency, then reserve actual delivery evidence for separately authorized integration work. No production credentials or private incident data are needed.

Each experiment keeps the current evaluator and baseline available. Any proposed evaluator change follows prior-evaluator review and dual-version replay; the candidate cannot promote itself. Rollback means stop candidate use, reconcile active runs and effects, restore the known-good artifact through the required corrective PR, and verify it before resuming. This report initiates none of those operational changes.
